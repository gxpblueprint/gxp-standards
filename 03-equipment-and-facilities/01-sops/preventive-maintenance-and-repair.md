# 03-SOP-PMR: Preventive Maintenance and Repair

## 1. Scope and prerequisites {#scope}

Use this SOP to plan preventive maintenance, respond to faults and return GMP equipment to controlled use. It covers manufacturing, quality-control and supporting equipment affecting quality or data for EU/US human finished medicinal products.

**Draft reference template:** Quality and the Equipment Owner must confirm site applicability, service capability, schedules and records before adoption. No site equipment history or operational evidence has been assessed. Local workflow controls supplement applicable requirements without creating universal service intervals.

Utility-system and facility qualification, process/cleaning validation, sterile or containment-specific designs, and API, veterinary and investigational-product specialties are excluded. Approved local safety, isolation, cleaning and line-clearance procedures are mandatory prerequisites. Stop affected work if those procedures or competent personnel are unavailable. This SOP does not authorise a safety bypass.

## 2. References and terms {#references}

- European Commission, [EU GMP Chapter 3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/18d76565-137b-41d2-a602-794527f708c1_en?filename=chapter_3.pdf), sections 3.34–3.44; operational March 2015.
- European Commission, [EU GMP Annex 15](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/7c6c5b3c-4902-46ea-b7ab-7608682fb68d_en?filename=2015-10_annex15.pdf), section 11.4; operational October 2015.
- FDA, [21 CFR 211.65](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.65), [211.67](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.67), [211.68](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.68) and [211.182](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.182).

**Preventive maintenance** is planned work intended to prevent deterioration or failure. **Repair** corrects a fault. **Return to use** is the documented authorisation to resume the stated GMP activity. **Quality** means the accountable Quality Unit.

## 3. Responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Equipment Owner | Maintain inventory, availability and visible operating status. |
| Engineering Owner | Approve technical work, schedules, parts and service-provider capability. |
| Technician | Follow approved work instructions and preserve actual findings. |
| Process Owner or Data Owner | Identify exposed work, records and operating requirements. |
| Independent verifier | Check completed work and return evidence independently of the executor. |
| Quality | Decide GMP impact, restrictions and return authorisation. |

Name trained deputies. Define supplier duties through supplier qualification and the work order. Supplier completion does not establish site GMP readiness.

## 4. Establish the maintenance programme {#programme}

1. Engineering must register equipment identity, location, intended use and quality-critical functions.
2. Assess failure modes using design information, usage, environment, manufacturer instructions and site history where available.
3. Define tasks, methods, disassembly/reassembly instructions, permitted parts, materials, frequencies and acceptance checks for each item.
4. Include performance inspections for automated equipment and interfaces where failures could affect GMP work. Coordinate calibration and qualification requirements with their owners.
5. Set due dates and escalation before work becomes overdue. Quality must approve the GMP programme and significant changes with Engineering.
6. Review new information before changing intervals. Do not backdate completion or extend a missed deadline retrospectively.

## 5. Prepare and protect the work area {#prepare}

1. Before intervention, the technician must confirm the equipment ID, fault or planned task, approved work scope and current status.
2. The Process Owner must identify active batches, materials, samples and records. Stop or segregate affected work before equipment is opened or settings are changed.
3. Obtain local safety and isolation authorisations. Protect adjacent equipment and exposed material from tools, debris, lubricants and cleaning agents.
4. Record the original condition, alarms, settings, damaged parts and available data before repair. Preserve evidence needed to establish when the fault began.
5. Verify approved parts, service instructions, calibrated test equipment and cleaning arrangements. Define objective functional and GMP return checks before execution.
6. If information is incomplete, stop the affected task and obtain clarification. Do not guess a replacement part or adjustment setting.

## 6. Execute authorised maintenance or repair {#execute}

1. The technician must perform the authorised steps and record actual work, personnel and times contemporaneously.
2. Record findings, parts replaced, part identification, materials used and settings before and after work. Assess product-contact suitability and contamination risk.
3. Keep operating substances from contaminating product or contact materials. Use only approved lubricants, coolants and replacement materials for the assessed application.
4. If additional work or a design/configuration change is needed, stop that activity and obtain technical and Quality assessment through change control.
5. Preserve removed components or representative evidence while they are needed for investigation. Record a justified disposition when physical retention is unsafe or impractical.
6. Reconcile tools, temporary connections, parts and cleaning materials before closing the equipment. Record any missing item and keep affected equipment unavailable.
7. For computerised interfaces or software, preserve configuration history and use CSV-defined change and verification controls.

## 7. Contain faults and overdue work {#defects}

1. If equipment fails, a due date is missed or a check fails, stop affected GMP use and mark the equipment unavailable.
2. Restrict access or isolate the item where a label alone cannot prevent use. Remove defective equipment from production or QC areas where practical.
3. Notify the Engineering Owner, Equipment Owner and Quality immediately when quality or data may be affected.
4. Open a deviation and record discovery time, known last use, symptoms and affected operations. Preserve logs, original settings and other available evidence.
5. Do not treat a restart, temporary repair or subsequent passing check as closure of the initial defect.

## 8. Assess historical impact {#impact}

1. Quality must direct the impact assessment with Engineering and the Process/Data Owner.
2. Identify the last supported acceptable state and earliest credible onset. Include the full potentially affected period through restriction and record uncertainty.
3. Trace affected products, batches, samples, measurements, records and downstream decisions. Consider contamination, loss of control, misleading measurements and incomplete data.
4. Assess related equipment or repeated service actions when a common part, setting or practice could share the defect.
5. Quality must hold decisions whose reliability is unresolved. Use OOS/OOT and quality-defect/recall procedures for affected laboratory or released-product concerns.
6. Document evidence supporting any exclusion from scope. Lack of a complaint or a later passing check is not proof of no impact.
7. Keep product/data disposition separate from technical repair. Define further investigation, CAPA and change actions with named owners.

## 9. Verify and authorise return to use {#return}

1. Engineering must confirm correct assembly, approved parts, removal of temporary connections and complete tool/material reconciliation.
2. Complete cleaning and line clearance under the approved local procedures. Remove previous batch or test identification while preserving its historical records.
3. Verify cleanliness immediately before use and protect clean equipment from contamination. Confirm the required clean, dry storage condition and any approved hold time.
4. Execute predefined functional, alarm, interlock and interface checks for the affected functions. Preserve failed checks and investigate before repeating them.
5. Obtain calibration or requalification where the intervention could affect measurement, operating range or qualified state. Use CSV for affected computerised functions.
6. The independent verifier must review the work order, original findings, test evidence, cleaning, calibration, qualification and linked deviations.
7. Quality must approve GMP return after evidence supports the intended use and historical-impact decisions or restrictions are documented. Keep unresolved affected product/data on hold.
8. Update equipment status, restrictions and next due work before notification to users. A completed work order alone does not authorise use.

## 10. Urgent and degraded work {#urgent}

For urgent repairs, Engineering must define the minimum safe scope and required acceptance checks before work. Obtain the same authorised technical and Quality decisions through named deputies. Do not use urgency to permit overdue or failed equipment, missing cleaning, or unverified temporary parts.

A temporary change requires documented limits, duration, monitoring, owner and recovery action under change control before use. It must not waive required performance or GMP release criteria.

If qualified personnel, approval or records are unavailable, stop the affected activity or transfer work to an already suitable qualified item. Use controlled paper work orders only when complete status and contemporaneous records can be maintained. Reconcile records after system recovery.

## 11. Records, closure and review {#review-records}

Engineering must retain work orders, schedules, original findings, parts/settings, performed and checked signatures, tests, deviations and Quality return authorisation. Maintain chronological equipment history with batch and record links. Document Control must apply the approved retention schedule.

For US major equipment, apply the equipment-log requirements of 21 CFR 211.182, including the stated routine-maintenance exception. Where dedicated equipment meets its numerical-sequence conditions, retain the required history in batch records. Record covered work with date, time, product/lot and dated performer/checker signatures. For compliant automated execution, retain the required human verification under 21 CFR 211.68. The broader maintenance-record programme still applies when the specific log exception is used.

Close work only after completion evidence and required disposition agree. Retain linked owners and due dates for follow-up actions. At the approved interval, Quality and Engineering must review scheduled/completed work, overdue tasks, repeat failures, emergency repairs and post-repair defects. Revise justified controls through change control and verify systemic actions through CAPA.
