# 03-SOP-EQ: Equipment Qualification

## 1. Scope and prerequisites {#scope}

Use this SOP to qualify new, changed, relocated or insufficiently evidenced GMP manufacturing and quality-control equipment. It covers equipment supporting EU/US human finished medicinal products, including measurement functions and computerised interfaces.

**Draft reference template:** Quality and the Equipment Owner must approve site applicability, responsibilities, limits and controlled records before adoption. No site equipment, procedures or operating evidence have been assessed. Applicable legal requirements remain mandatory; the detailed workflow includes local control choices.

Utility-system and facility qualification, process and cleaning validation, sterile or containment-specific designs, and API, veterinary and investigational-product specialties are excluded. Follow the approved local safety, equipment cleaning and line-clearance procedures as prerequisites. Stop affected work if those prerequisites are absent. Route computerised functions through the existing CSV procedure.

## 2. References and terms {#references}

- European Commission, [EU GMP Chapter 3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/18d76565-137b-41d2-a602-794527f708c1_en?filename=chapter_3.pdf), sections 3.34–3.41; operational March 2015.
- European Commission, [EU GMP Annex 15](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/7c6c5b3c-4902-46ea-b7ab-7608682fb68d_en?filename=2015-10_annex15.pdf), General and sections 1–4 and 11; operational October 2015.
- FDA, [21 CFR 211.63](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.63), [211.65](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.65) and [211.68](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.68), as applicable.

**Qualification** demonstrates fitness for defined equipment use. A **user requirements specification (URS)** states that use and its requirements. **Quality** means the accountable Quality Unit. The stages below may use combined documents when the approved risk assessment supports the approach.

## 3. Responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Equipment Owner | Own the inventory entry, intended use, operating controls and equipment status. |
| Qualification Lead | Plan and coordinate evidence, traceability, tests and reports. |
| Engineering | Verify design, installation, service requirements and supplier evidence. |
| Process Owner or QC representative | Define actual operating ranges, loads and measurement needs. |
| Quality | Approve the strategy, critical criteria, deviations and GMP release independently of the executor. |
| Trained users and testers | Execute approved steps and preserve all results. |

Name trained deputies before testing. Supplier personnel may execute agreed work; supplier sign-off does not replace site review or Quality disposition.

## 4. Plan and specify {#plan}

1. The Equipment Owner must register a unique equipment ID, location, model, configuration and proposed GMP use.
2. The Qualification Lead must assess quality and data risks with users and Engineering. Identify functions, interfaces and failures that could affect GMP decisions.
3. Prepare the URS and qualification plan within the site validation master plan or equivalent programme.
4. Specify intended ranges, loads, materials, measurement capability, alarms, interfaces and records. Define cleaning access and operating-environment requirements.
5. Map each critical requirement to a planned verification and objective acceptance criterion. Define sample sizes, repetitions and challenge conditions with a scientific rationale.
6. Define design, installation, operation and performance stages, supplier contributions, prerequisites and release gates. Justify combined stages or omitted tests with equivalent evidence; simplicity alone does not remove a critical check.
7. Obtain Quality and relevant functional approvals before execution. Do not write acceptance limits after observing test results.

## 5. Review design and supplier evidence {#design-vendor}

1. Engineering must compare the proposed design against the approved URS. Check size, location, product-contact materials and access for operation, cleaning and maintenance.
2. Assess lubricants, coolants and other operating substances for possible product contamination. Resolve incompatible materials before acceptance.
3. Verify measurement ranges and precision against the intended decision. Route instrument calibration through Calibration Management.
4. The Qualification Lead must review supplier protocols, configurations, raw results, deviations, test instruments and approval history before relying on them.
5. Record accepted supplier evidence and remaining gaps. Supplier certification or a commercial acceptance statement alone is not qualification evidence.
6. Where factory acceptance testing replaces a site test, justify why transport and installation cannot affect that function. Perform site acceptance or repeat affected tests when that justification is unsupported.
7. Confirm supplier competence and responsibilities through supplier qualification. Resolve changed specifications through change control.

## 6. Verify installation {#installation}

1. Engineering must verify the delivered equipment identity and condition against the approved order and design.
2. Record installed location, components, configuration, drawings, product-contact materials and service connections. Confirm service availability against the equipment requirements without claiming utility-system qualification under this SOP.
3. Obtain operating, maintenance and cleaning instructions. Confirm safe access and the required local safety authorisations.
4. Verify current calibration of instruments used to control the equipment or produce qualification evidence.
5. Compare installation with predefined criteria and record discrepancies. Retain evidence of corrections and the affected repeat checks.
6. The Qualification Lead must obtain the documented installation-stage decision before dependent tests proceed.

## 7. Verify operation and performance {#operation-performance}

1. Execute operational qualification using the approved protocol and installed configuration. Record actual conditions and original observations at the time of testing.
2. Challenge intended operating limits, justified worst cases, alarms and failure responses. Verify critical interfaces and calculations through the linked CSV evidence.
3. Use performance qualification to demonstrate reproducible intended operation with approved representative loads, materials and operating ranges.
4. Justify substitutes or simulations by their relevant behaviour. A convenient test material is not sufficient without a documented basis.
5. Record every run, interruption, repeat and deviation. Preserve failed results and the relationship between original and repeat tests.
6. Compare results with each criterion. Finalise operating and cleaning instructions, training and maintenance requirements from the demonstrated conditions.

## 8. Investigate failures and evidence gaps {#failures}

1. If a criterion fails, stop the affected test or dependent activity and preserve equipment status and original evidence.
2. Open a deviation. Quality and the Qualification Lead must assess the cause, affected requirements and implications for earlier or later tests.
3. If equipment has already supported GMP work, identify the full potentially affected period, products, batches and data. Begin from the last supported acceptable state or earlier credible onset; record uncertainty.
4. Quality must hold affected decisions where reliability is unresolved. Use the deviation, OOS/OOT and quality-defect procedures as applicable.
5. Authorise correction and justified repeat testing through the investigation and change procedures. Do not repeat testing solely to obtain a passing result. Do not remove inconvenient results.
6. Document significant protocol changes and scientific justification. Do not widen criteria merely to accept a failure.
7. For legacy equipment, assess existing evidence and prospectively close gaps. Historical use alone does not establish qualification or legitimise past unsupported use.

## 9. Authorise stages and GMP release {#release}

1. The Qualification Lead must report results against requirements and acceptance criteria, including failures, changes and remaining actions.
2. Quality and the relevant stage owner must authorise progression. Conditional progression may address a defined open item only when documented assessment shows no significant impact on the next activity.
3. State the exact permitted next-stage activity, restrictions, owner and completion trigger. Such permission does not authorise routine GMP use or batch release.
4. Before GMP release, the Equipment Owner must confirm completed required qualification, calibration, maintenance arrangements, trained users and approved operating instructions.
5. Confirm cleaning, removal of previous test or batch identification, line clearance and protection from contamination under the local procedures.
6. Quality must independently review the release package and decide GMP status. Do not release while an unresolved item affects intended-use acceptance or record reliability.
7. Record permitted use and restrictions in the equipment register. Apply matching status identification before users receive access.

## 10. Urgent or degraded work {#urgent}

If an approver is absent, use the named authorised deputy. If no authorised decision-maker is available, keep the affected activity on hold.

For urgent demand, prioritise review or use another qualified item with confirmed method and process suitability. Do not bypass tests, release criteria or safety controls. Record the decision and any temporary change through the governing procedure.

If the normal record system is unavailable, use approved controlled paper records with contemporaneous entries. Reconcile them into the controlled file when access returns. Stop testing if complete evidence cannot be retained.

## 11. Lifecycle review and records {#review-records}

The Equipment Owner must set a Quality-approved review interval from criticality, usage, failure history and change rate. Review also after relocation, significant repair, altered use, unexplained performance or cumulative minor changes.

Assess qualification, calibration, maintenance, deviations and data trends together. Define and approve any requalification scope and criteria before testing. Retire superseded configurations through change control while retaining their history.

Retain the URS, design review, supplier assessment, protocols, original data, deviations, reports, stage decisions and GMP release in the controlled equipment file. Document Control must apply the approved retention schedule and retrievable links to affected batches and records.

At the approved interval, Quality and Engineering must review failed tests, overdue reviews, recurring defects and unauthorised use. Assign actions and effectiveness checks through CAPA.
