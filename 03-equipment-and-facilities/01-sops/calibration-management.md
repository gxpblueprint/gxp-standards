# 03-SOP-CAL: Calibration Management

## 1. Scope and prerequisites {#scope}

Use this SOP to control calibration of measuring, weighing, recording and control equipment that affects GMP product quality or data. It covers manufacturing and quality-control equipment for EU/US human finished medicinal products.

**Draft reference template:** Quality and the Equipment Owner must confirm local applicability, measurement needs, standards, intervals and records before adoption. No site inventory or metrology evidence has been assessed. Detailed workflow choices supplement applicable requirements; no universal frequency or tolerance is specified.

Utility or facility qualification, process/cleaning validation and sterile, containment, API, veterinary or investigational-product specialties are excluded. Follow approved local safety and equipment-specific instructions before work. Stop if those prerequisites are unavailable. Coordinate computerised measurement functions through CSV.

## 2. References and terms {#references}

- European Commission, [EU GMP Chapter 3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/18d76565-137b-41d2-a602-794527f708c1_en?filename=chapter_3.pdf), sections 3.40, 3.41 and 3.44; operational March 2015.
- European Commission, [EU GMP Annex 15](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/7c6c5b3c-4902-46ea-b7ab-7608682fb68d_en?filename=2015-10_annex15.pdf), sections 3.9 and 11.4; operational October 2015.
- FDA, [21 CFR 211.160](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-I/section-211.160), paragraphs (a) and (b)(4), [211.194(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-J/section-211.194), and [211.68(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-D/section-211.68).

**Calibration** establishes the relationship between indication and reference values under specified conditions. **Adjustment** changes equipment to obtain required performance. **As-found** results describe performance before adjustment; **as-left** results describe performance after the intervention. **Quality** means the accountable Quality Unit.

## 3. Responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Calibration Owner | Maintain methods, standards, schedules and technical assessments. |
| Equipment Owner | Provide intended use, equipment availability and controlled status. |
| Calibration technician | Execute the approved method and retain all observations. |
| Independent verifier | Check results, calculations, criteria and evidence completeness. |
| Process Owner or Data Owner | Identify affected processes, batches, records and decisions. |
| Quality | Approve the programme, failure disposition and GMP return independently of the executor. |

Name trained deputies. Assess external calibration providers through supplier qualification. A service certificate does not replace the site's acceptance decision.

## 4. Establish the calibration programme {#programme}

1. The Calibration Owner must assign each item a unique ID and identify its GMP measurement purpose, range and required precision.
2. Define calibration points, method, environment, accuracy and precision limits, and the decision rule for acceptance. Address measurement uncertainty relative to intended-use limits.
3. Specify reference standards and their required traceability to recognised national or international standards. If that traceability is unavailable, justify a technically valid reference basis before use.
4. Set intervals from criticality, usage, environment, manufacturer information, drift and failure history. Define due dates and intermediate checks where justified.
5. Distinguish calibration from routine verification checks. A passing routine check does not extend calibration validity without an approved reassessment before the due date.
6. Obtain Quality approval of the programme and changes. Prohibit retrospective date extensions and use after the approved due date.
7. Link calibration requirements to equipment qualification, maintenance and CSV configuration control.

## 5. Prepare for calibration {#prepare}

1. Before the due date, arrange equipment availability and identify dependent work that must stop or move to a suitable alternative.
2. The technician must confirm equipment identity, configuration and status against the work request.
3. Verify the approved method, reference-standard identity, valid calibration, range and uncertainty. Check storage, handling and environmental conditions that could invalidate the standard.
4. Record initial condition, damage, seals and relevant settings before intervention. Preserve available evidence if a fault prevents measurement.
5. Identify test software and calculation versions where used. Complete local safety authorisation and contamination controls before handling equipment.
6. If a prerequisite is missing or contradictory, stop and resolve it with the Calibration Owner. Do not adjust the item to make preparation appear acceptable.

## 6. Obtain as-found results {#as-found}

1. Perform the approved measurement sequence before adjustment, repair or configuration changes that could alter the result.
2. Record reference values, indications, units, test points, repeats, environment, time and operator contemporaneously. Retain original electronic data and metadata where generated.
3. Calculate error and assess accuracy, precision and uncertainty under the approved decision rule.
4. Retain every result, including failures and interrupted runs. Do not replace a failed value with a later passing value.
5. If as-found measurement is impossible or unsafe, record why and preserve remaining condition evidence. Refer the missing information to Quality for impact assessment; do not invent a result.
6. If a limit fails, restrict the equipment immediately and start the failure assessment below before any return-to-use decision.

## 7. Adjust, repair and establish as-left performance {#adjustment}

1. Obtain the authorised adjustment or repair instruction. Use change control when settings, components, software or intended performance could affect the approved state.
2. Record what changed and link the intervention to the original as-found evidence. Apply the maintenance procedure for repair work.
3. Perform the approved as-left sequence over the required range. Include checks affected by the intervention and retain all repeats.
4. The verifier must compare results with the original approved criteria and decision rule. Do not widen tolerances to secure a pass.
5. If as-left performance fails, keep the item unavailable and investigate. A passing as-left result does not resolve historical as-found failure impact.

## 8. Assess failures, overdue items and reference defects {#failure}

1. On failure or overdue status, the Equipment Owner must stop GMP use and identify the item as unavailable. Prevent access where a label alone is insufficient.
2. Notify Quality and the Calibration Owner immediately when affected measurements may have supported GMP work. Open a deviation and preserve records.
3. Determine the last supported acceptable state and earliest credible onset of the defect. Include every potentially affected measurement through restriction, not only the last batch.
4. Evaluate error direction and magnitude, uncertainty, range, drift, intermediate checks and actual use. Do not assume the failure began on the calibration date.
5. The Process/Data Owner must identify affected batches, tests, release decisions and downstream calculations. Quality must hold unresolved decisions and route released-product concerns through quality-defect procedures.
6. If a reference standard is defective, assess all equipment calibrated with it during the potentially affected period. Extend the review to their product and data decisions.
7. Record the rationale and limits of any narrowed impact window. Where evidence cannot support exclusion, retain the item or record in scope.
8. Quality must disposition historical impact separately from equipment repair. Use OOS/OOT investigation rules where laboratory results are affected.

## 9. Verify and authorise return to use {#return}

1. The independent verifier must check equipment identity, method, standard validity, raw results, calculations, uncertainty, criteria and the complete intervention history.
2. Check required cleaning, maintenance, configuration and requalification evidence. Reconcile missing or conflicting certificate information with the provider before acceptance.
3. Quality must approve GMP return only after current performance is acceptable and historical-impact decisions or justified restrictions are documented.
4. Keep unresolved affected product or data on hold under its governing procedure. Equipment return does not release a batch.
5. Record the authorisation, approved range, restrictions and next due date. Apply consistent equipment and register status before use.

## 10. Urgent and degraded conditions {#urgent}

For an urgent measurement, use another qualified, calibrated item only after the Process Owner confirms suitability for the method and range. Record any required change approval. Do not use an overdue or failed item because production is waiting.

If the technician, verifier or Quality approver is absent, use the authorised trained deputy. Otherwise keep the item unavailable. Prioritise work by quality risk without removing acceptance steps.

If electronic scheduling or records are unavailable, use approved controlled paper records and a checked due-date list. Reconcile the records after recovery. Stop work when status, standards or complete evidence cannot be confirmed.

## 11. Records and programme review {#review-records}

The Calibration Owner must retain methods, schedules, complete raw results, certificates, reference-standard evidence, adjustments, failures, investigations and return authorisations in the controlled calibration history. Link the history to equipment and affected batches or data. Document Control must apply the approved retention schedule.

At the approved review interval, compare scheduled and completed calibrations, overdue items, as-found failures, repeated adjustments and drift. Review provider quality and reference-standard failures. Quality must approve justified interval or method changes through change control before application. Assign recurring causes and effectiveness checks through CAPA.
