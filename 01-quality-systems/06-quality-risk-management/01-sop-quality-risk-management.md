# 01-SOP-QRM: Quality Risk Management

> **Use this SOP to:** make documented quality-risk decisions with defined criteria, controls, owners, and review triggers.

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines how Quality Risk Management (QRM) is applied across the Pharmaceutical Quality System.

The process supports risk-based decisions that are:

- based on scientific knowledge and process experience
- proportionate to importance, uncertainty, and complexity
- documented at the right level of formality
- reviewed when knowledge or conditions change

## 2. Scope

QRM may be used for:

- deviation, CAPA, change control, validation, cleaning, process, laboratory, supplier, audit, data integrity, computerized system, and training decisions
- risk acceptance and residual risk review
- prioritization of actions and resources
- management review and continual improvement
- product availability risk linked to manufacturing quality issues

## 3. References

- ICH Q9(R1), Quality Risk Management.
- ICH Q10, Pharmaceutical Quality System.
- FDA Guidance for Industry: Quality Systems Approach to Pharmaceutical CGMP Regulations.
- EU GMP Chapter 1, Pharmaceutical Quality System.
- EU GMP Annex 15, Qualification and Validation.
- ISPE GAMP 5, Second Edition, for computerized-system risk-based validation principles.

## 4. Principles

- Risk evaluation must be based on scientific knowledge and ultimately linked to protection of the patient.
- The level of effort, formality, and documentation must be commensurate with the level of risk.
- Higher uncertainty, importance, or complexity requires more structured QRM.
- Resource constraints alone must not justify accepting unacceptable quality risk.
- Subjectivity must be managed through defined criteria, cross-functional review, and clear rationale.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Quality Unit | Owns QRM procedure, approves high-risk decisions and risk acceptance, ensures QRM quality. |
| Risk Owner | Defines the decision, coordinates assessment, tracks actions, and maintains the risk record. |
| SMEs | Provide process, product, validation, engineering, lab, supplier, IT, regulatory, and data integrity knowledge. |
| Senior Management | Reviews significant residual risks, provides resources, and resolves escalations. |
| Facilitator | Guides formal risk workshops and ensures consistent method use. |

## 6. Choosing Formality

| Situation | Appropriate formality |
| --- | --- |
| Low importance, low uncertainty, simple decision | Brief documented rationale or checklist. |
| Moderate GMP impact or some uncertainty | Structured risk assessment with defined criteria and Quality review. |
| High patient/product/data integrity impact, high uncertainty, complex or cross-functional decision | Formal tool, cross-functional team, approved report, risk controls, effectiveness review, and management escalation where needed. |

## 6.1 QRM Operating Sequence

1. State the decision or risk question.
2. Define scope, assumptions, and what is out of scope.
3. Select the formality level from Section 6.
4. Identify hazards and failure modes.
5. Rate or describe risk using defined criteria.
6. Select controls and assign owners/due dates.
7. Decide whether residual risk is acceptable.
8. Obtain Quality approval for GMP-significant decisions.
9. Define review triggers.
10. Reopen the assessment when assumptions change or new data contradict the decision.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Define the risk question

State:

- decision to be made
- process, product, system, supplier, or record in scope
- assumptions and boundaries
- available knowledge
- affected patient/product/data integrity risk
- decision owner and approver

### 7.2 Identify hazards

Identify what could go wrong using:

- process maps
- historical deviations/OOS/OOT/complaints
- audit and inspection findings
- supplier history
- validation and monitoring data
- scientific knowledge
- human factors and use conditions
- data integrity and computerized system risks

### 7.3 Analyze risk

Assess severity, probability, and detectability where useful. Not every decision requires numerical scoring. If scoring is used, define criteria before scoring.

Consider:

- patient impact
- product quality impact
- data integrity impact
- regulatory commitment impact
- process control and detectability
- uncertainty in knowledge
- recurrence or trend
- product availability impact from quality failures

### 7.4 Evaluate risk

Compare risk against predefined criteria or documented acceptance rationale.

Risk decisions may include:

- acceptable as-is
- acceptable with controls
- unacceptable until controls are implemented
- additional data required before decision
- escalate to management or Quality Council

### 7.5 Control risk

Risk controls may include:

- process or design change
- specification, method, or procedure update
- additional verification or monitoring
- training or qualification
- supplier control
- technical system control
- segregation, hold, or inspection
- CAPA or change control

Controls must be assigned to owners with due dates and evidence expectations.

### 7.6 Accept residual risk

Residual risk may be accepted only when:

- required controls are implemented or scheduled with interim controls
- patient safety and product quality remain protected
- acceptance rationale is documented
- Quality approves the decision
- senior management approves significant business or product availability risk where appropriate

### 7.7 Communicate and review

Communicate risk decisions to affected functions. Review risk assessments when:

- new knowledge emerges
- deviation, complaint, audit, or trend contradicts assumptions
- process, supplier, system, method, or regulation changes
- periodic review is due
- residual risk remains high

## 8. Common QRM Tools

| Tool | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Checklist / decision tree | Simple repeatable decisions. |
| FMEA | Process, equipment, system, or method failure modes. |
| HACCP | Contamination or process-control hazards. |
| Fault tree | Investigating or modelling top-event causes. |
| Risk matrix | Severity/probability prioritization. |
| Bow-tie | Visualizing preventive and detective controls. |
| Preliminary hazard analysis | Early project or supplier risk screening. |

Tools support judgment; they do not replace evidence or Quality accountability.

## 9. Records

Risk records must include:

- risk question and scope
- team and date
- method and criteria
- hazards and risk analysis
- controls and owners
- residual risk and acceptance
- approvals
- review date or trigger

## 10. Metrics

Quality should trend:

- high residual risks
- overdue risk-control actions
- repeated risk assessments for same issue
- risk assessments later contradicted by deviations or complaints
- risk-based decisions escalated to management

## 11. Quick Checklist

- Is the decision clearly stated?
- Is formality appropriate to importance, uncertainty, and complexity?
- Is scientific/process knowledge used?
- Are assumptions visible?
- Are controls realistic and assigned?
- Is residual risk approved at the right level?
- Is a review trigger defined?

*This SOP aligns with ICH Q9(R1) expectations for formality, risk-based decision-making, subjectivity control, and risk review, and with ICH Q10 expectations for QRM as an enabler of the Pharmaceutical Quality System.*
