# 01-SOP-AUD: Internal Audit and Self-Inspection

> **Use this SOP to:** plan, conduct, report, correct, and close internal audits/self-inspections.

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the internal audit/self-inspection program used to verify GMP compliance, identify quality system weaknesses, drive corrective action, and support continual improvement.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to internal audits of:

- manufacturing, packaging, warehouse, laboratory, QA, engineering, maintenance, validation, computerized systems, and support areas
- documentation, training, deviation, CAPA, change control, supplier qualification, data integrity, complaints, recalls, and management review systems
- outsourced activities where internal audit verifies oversight processes

Supplier audits are managed under the Supplier Qualification SOP, but supplier-audit trends may feed the internal audit program.

## 3. References

- FDA 21 CFR 211.22, 211.25, 211.100, 211.180, 211.192.
- FDA Guidance for Industry: Quality Systems Approach to Pharmaceutical CGMP Regulations.
- EU GMP Chapter 1, Pharmaceutical Quality System.
- EU GMP Chapter 9, Self Inspection.
- ICH Q9(R1), Quality Risk Management.
- ICH Q10, Pharmaceutical Quality System.

## 4. Principles

- Audits must be independent enough to be objective.
- Audit scope and frequency should be risk-based.
- Audit reports must be factual, clear, and linked to evidence.
- Findings must be corrected through CAPA or documented correction, not left as informal observations.
- Audit records should be protected and distributed according to site policy.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Quality Unit | Owns the audit program, approves schedule, assigns auditors, approves reports, tracks CAPA, and escalates overdue actions. |
| Auditor | Prepares audit plan, conducts audit, records evidence, classifies findings, writes report, and verifies responses. |
| Auditee / Area Owner | Provides access to records and personnel, responds to findings, owns corrections/CAPA, and supplies completion evidence. |
| Senior Management | Reviews audit trends, removes barriers, and ensures adequate resources. |
| SME | Supports technical review where specialized expertise is needed. |

## 6. Audit Program

Create an annual audit schedule that covers all GMP systems over a defined cycle. Higher-risk areas may be audited more frequently.

Scheduling factors:

- product and process criticality
- prior audit findings and CAPA performance
- deviation, complaint, OOS/OOT, recall, or inspection history
- new products, processes, equipment, systems, suppliers, or facilities
- data integrity risk
- management review input
- regulatory commitments

The schedule may be adjusted when risk changes.

## 7. Audit Types

| Type | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Routine system audit | Planned audit of a quality system or operational area. |
| For-cause audit | Triggered by deviation trend, complaint, inspection finding, data integrity concern, or management request. |
| Readiness audit | Performed before regulatory inspection, launch, validation milestone, or major change. |
| Follow-up audit | Confirms effectiveness of corrective actions or remediation. |

## 7.1 Audit Sequence

1. Approve the annual or for-cause audit scope.
2. Assign an auditor independent of the work being audited where practical.
3. Prepare the audit plan and record sample.
4. Conduct the opening meeting.
5. Review records, interview personnel, and observe the process.
6. Classify findings using the criteria in Section 8.4.
7. Escalate critical findings immediately.
8. Issue the report with evidence and due dates.
9. Approve responses and CAPA.
10. Verify completion and close findings.

## 8. Procedure

### 8.1 Prepare

The auditor prepares an audit plan including:

- audit objective and scope
- area/process/system audited
- auditor(s) and SMEs
- dates and agenda
- documents and records to sample
- applicable regulations, SOPs, specifications, and prior commitments
- previous audit findings and open CAPA
- high-risk topics requiring focused review

### 8.2 Conduct opening meeting

Confirm:

- scope and agenda
- personnel needed
- record access
- safety or confidentiality requirements
- how potential critical findings will be escalated during the audit

### 8.3 Perform audit

Use interviews, observation, record review, data review, and sampling.

Audit evidence may include:

- SOPs, forms, logbooks, batch records, lab records, training records
- deviation, CAPA, change control, complaint, supplier, validation, and maintenance records
- computerized system access, audit trail, backup, and data review records
- physical area observations
- trend reports and management review inputs

Record evidence clearly enough that another reviewer can understand the basis of each finding.

### 8.4 Classify findings

| Classification | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Deficiency with actual or likely patient safety, product quality, data integrity, regulatory, or released-product impact. Requires immediate Quality escalation and containment. |
| Major | Significant GMP weakness or repeated minor weakness that could affect quality if not corrected. Requires CAPA or justified correction plan. |
| Minor | Isolated GMP weakness with limited risk and no direct quality impact. Requires correction and trend review. |
| Opportunity | Improvement suggestion where no GMP nonconformance is identified. Optional unless accepted by management. |

### 8.5 Report

Issue an audit report containing:

- audit objective, scope, dates, auditor(s), auditees
- areas and records sampled
- summary of strengths and risks
- findings with evidence, classification, and requirement reference
- immediate containment required, if any
- response due dates
- CAPA or correction requirements

Reports should be issued within a defined timeframe, usually 15-30 calendar days.

### 8.6 Respond and follow up

The Area Owner provides:

- correction or containment already completed
- root cause assessment for critical/major findings
- CAPA plan with owner and due date
- effectiveness check where required

Quality reviews and approves responses. Overdue responses or actions are escalated.

### 8.7 Close

Close findings when:

- correction/CAPA evidence is adequate
- required effectiveness check is complete or scheduled
- residual risk is acceptable
- Quality approves closure

## 9. Auditor Qualification

Auditors must be trained and competent in:

- GMP requirements and site procedures
- audit techniques
- evidence sampling
- finding classification
- objective report writing
- confidentiality and professional conduct

Auditors should not audit their own direct work unless independence is otherwise justified and approved.

## 10. Records

Maintain:

- annual audit schedule
- audit plans and checklists
- attendance records
- audit notes or evidence logs
- reports
- finding responses
- CAPA and effectiveness evidence
- trend reports

## 11. Metrics and Management Review

Trend:

- audits completed versus planned
- critical/major/minor findings
- repeat findings
- overdue responses and CAPA
- time to closure
- findings by system, area, process, supplier, and data integrity topic

Audit trends must feed management review and risk-based audit planning.

## 12. Quick Checklist

Before closing an audit:

- Scope was completed or deviations from scope justified.
- Findings are evidence-based and classified.
- Critical findings were escalated immediately.
- Responses include owners and due dates.
- CAPA is linked where required.
- Effectiveness checks are defined for significant findings.
