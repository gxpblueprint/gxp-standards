# 01-SOP-CAPA: Corrective and Preventive Action Management

> **Use this SOP to:** turn investigation findings and emerging risks into verified, effective corrective and preventive actions.

:::caution Draft reference — adapt before use
This is a reference draft, not an approved site procedure. Quality and the Process Owner must confirm applicability, roles, records and operating arrangements before adoption. Complete a qualified user walkthrough and approval before operational use.
:::

## 1. Purpose and scope {#scope}

GMP actions arising from deviations, complaints, OOS/OOT investigations, audits, product reviews, supplier issues and adverse trends. The source process retains investigation and product-disposition ownership.

The instructions below are proposed GxP Blueprint controls for adoption. Regulatory requirements apply only within their legal scope. Guidance informs the design; it does not make every implementation choice a legal obligation. Sources were checked on 8 September 2026. Veterinary, investigational and specialist product requirements need separate assessment.

Quality oversight, approved procedures and investigation follow-up; these sections do not prescribe a universal CAPA form or deadline.

- [Change Control](./change-control.md): Approval and verification of planned GMP changes.
- [Management Review](./management-review.md): Resources and systemic escalation.
- [Training Management](./training-management.md): Training and qualification before changed work.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| CAPA Coordinator | Maintains the CAPA register, links source events and escalates missed dates. |
| Action Owner | Designs and implements the assigned action and supplies completion evidence. |
| Process Owner | Confirms causal rationale, resources and practical implementation. |
| Effectiveness Reviewer | Reviews evidence independently of implementing the action. |
| Quality Unit | Approves the plan, extensions, effectiveness conclusions and closure. |
| Senior Management | Resolves resource constraints and repeated failures without overriding Quality disposition. |

Quality Unit means the adopting organisation's authorised quality function; US quality control unit responsibilities remain with that function. Appoint qualified deputies with documented authority. One person may hold compatible roles; preserve the independent checks required below.

## 4. Procedure {#procedure}

### 4.1. Approve the operating arrangements {#readiness}

1. Quality Unit: approve a CAPA record template and register before this procedure takes effect.
2. Define source-event links, severity criteria, deputies, escalation contacts and controlled record storage.
3. Set initiation, implementation and effectiveness-review dates from risk, exposure and action complexity.
4. Define escalation before a missed date can remove a needed interim control.
5. Train owners and reviewers before assigning independent work.

Use these states: proposed, approved, implementing, awaiting effectiveness, closed, and cancelled with rationale. Keep an audit history of state changes. Cancellation must retain the original commitment and Quality-approved reason.

### 4.2. Open and assess a CAPA {#intake}

1. CAPA Coordinator: register the source event and the problem the action must address.
2. Link related events instead of opening competing action plans for the same cause.
3. Process Owner: assess recurrence, affected products, sites, systems and the consequences of ineffective action.
4. Record the confirmed cause, supporting evidence and remaining uncertainty from the source investigation.
5. If the cause is uncertain, define additional investigation and protective controls before proposing a final solution.
6. Quality Unit: approve the CAPA scope, priority, owner and target dates.

If patient, product or data risk is uncontrolled, notify Quality immediately. Maintain containment through the source procedure. Do not wait for CAPA approval to protect affected product or records.

If a separate CAPA is not warranted, Quality must record why correction and source-event follow-up are sufficient. Reassess that decision if the issue recurs.

### 4.3. Approve actions and effectiveness criteria {#plan}

1. Action Owner: define each action, the cause or risk addressed, deliverable, owner and due date.
2. Distinguish immediate correction from corrective action and preventive action.
3. Process Owner: check whether the action introduces another failure mode or shifts risk elsewhere.
4. Link required change controls, validation, supplier commitments and training.
5. Effectiveness Reviewer: define the population, sampling rationale, observation period, data source and measurable success criteria before implementation.
6. Define what constitutes failure, insufficient exposure and an inconclusive review.
7. Quality Unit: approve the plan before planned GMP changes begin.

Do not select retraining as the default action. Use training only when evidence identifies a knowledge or skill gap. Define how competence will be demonstrated.

Example: for a revised label-verification control, specify the checks and representative opportunities needed to show the failure is controlled. Zero reported errors without sufficient production exposure does not demonstrate effectiveness.

### 4.4. Implement and verify completion {#implementation}

1. Action Owner: execute each approved action through the linked controlled process.
2. Record the actual completion date and attach identifiable evidence.
3. Verify revised documents, qualification and training before changed work starts.
4. Process Owner: compare deliverables with the approved plan.
5. If an action differs from the plan, obtain Quality approval of the revision before relying on it.
6. Keep the original plan, reason for revision and affected effectiveness criteria.
7. Quality Unit: move the CAPA to awaiting effectiveness only after implementation evidence is accepted.

Implementation completion does not prove effectiveness. The source investigation may close under its own criteria with an open linked CAPA. The CAPA remains traceable and open until its own closure criteria are met.

### 4.5. Evaluate effectiveness and close {#effectiveness}

1. Effectiveness Reviewer: retrieve the approved criteria and underlying data.
2. Confirm the planned population and exposure were achieved.
3. Check recurrence, unintended effects and whether the action controls the identified cause.
4. Record effective, ineffective or inconclusive, with the supporting evidence.
5. If ineffective, notify Quality and reopen the causal assessment.
6. If inconclusive, document the missing evidence and a risk-based further review plan.
7. Quality Unit: approve closure only when actions are complete and effectiveness criteria are met.
8. Record the closure decision, reviewer, date and links to continuing monitoring.

An ineffective action requires reassessment of affected product, systems and previously closed source events. Do not rename the same failed action to conceal recurrence. Preserve the original CAPA and link any replacement.

### 4.6. Handle urgent work, delay and loss of the normal system {#exceptions}

1. If immediate containment is needed, follow Deviation Handling and document the event contemporaneously.
2. Use Change Control for emergency changes and its retrospective review requirements.
3. Before a due date is missed, Action Owner: request an extension with cause, progress, risk and revised date.
4. Quality Unit: approve an extension only when interim controls remain effective.
5. If a date has passed, record the overdue condition and escalate without backdating approval.
6. If controls fail or resources remain unavailable, escalate to Senior Management and maintain holds where needed.
7. During a record-system outage, use the approved numbered paper record and record actual times and signatures.
8. After restoration, reconcile paper and electronic entries by unique ID and have a second trained person check completeness.

A deputy must hold the required authority and competence. Workload, absence and urgency do not permit an owner to approve their own effectiveness assessment.

### 4.7. Retain the CAPA record and review performance {#records}

CAPA Coordinator: retain the following in the controlled CAPA file as work occurs:

- Unique ID, source links, affected scope, priority and cause evidence.
- Approved actions, owners, dates, interim controls and revision history.
- Implementation evidence, change and training links.
- Effectiveness protocol, raw evidence, reviewer conclusion and Quality closure.
- Extensions, escalations, cancelled commitments and replacement CAPA links.

Quality Unit: approve a review frequency based on severity, workload and recurrence. At each review, assess overdue actions, extension frequency, failed or inconclusive effectiveness checks and repeat events. Escalate any loss of risk control immediately. Send systemic findings and resource needs to Management Review.

Apply Document Control retention rules to linked records. Prevent disposal while investigations, legal holds or linked product-retention obligations remain open.

## 5. Adoption and closure checklist {#checklist}

- Confirm: Approved roles, records, state model and risk-based dates exist before use.
- Confirm: Every intake has source evidence, scope, owner, containment and a justified action decision.
- Confirm: Actions address evidenced causes and have prospective measurable verification criteria.
- Confirm: Completion is evidenced separately from effectiveness, with controlled changes and retained history.
- Confirm: Closure requires independent evidence review; failure and uncertainty cause reassessment.
- Confirm: Urgency and delays preserve protection, independent authority and record continuity.
- Confirm: A reviewer can reconstruct each decision and management can detect ineffective or overdue controls.
