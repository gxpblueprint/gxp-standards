# 01-SOP-BRR: Batch Record Review and Release

> **Use this SOP to:** review batch evidence, resolve quality questions and authorise the correct release status before sale or distribution.

:::caution Draft reference — adapt before use
This is a reference draft, not an approved site procedure. Quality and the Process Owner must confirm applicability, roles, records and operating arrangements before adoption. Complete a qualified user walkthrough and approval before operational use.
:::

## 1. Purpose and scope {#scope}

Human finished medicinal-product batch review and disposition. EU QP certification and US Quality Unit release have distinct legal bases. Product-specific manufacturing, import testing, official-control release, real-time or parametric release, investigational products, biological products and sterile release require additional approved procedures where applicable.

The instructions below are proposed GxP Blueprint controls for adoption. Regulatory requirements apply only within their legal scope. Guidance informs the design; it does not make every implementation choice a legal obligation. Sources were checked on 8 September 2026. Veterinary, investigational and specialist product requirements need separate assessment.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Production / Packaging Owner | Submits complete manufacturing and packaging records and resolves queries with attributable evidence. |
| Quality Control Laboratory | Provides reviewed results, complete investigation links and applicable certificates. |
| Quality Reviewer | Examines the dossier and records deficiencies and their resolution. |
| Quality Unit | Approves or rejects product within its authority and maintains holds while evidence is unresolved. |
| Qualified Person, where applicable | Performs EU certification within the relevant authorisation and records it in the register or equivalent record. |
| Regulatory Affairs | Confirms market, authorisation and commitment requirements. |
| Distribution Owner | Transfers only correctly authorised product to saleable status and verifies downstream status. |

Quality Unit means the adopting organisation's authorised quality function; US quality control unit responsibilities remain with that function. Appoint qualified deputies with documented authority. One person may hold compatible roles; preserve the independent checks required below.

## 4. Procedure {#procedure}

### 4.1. Approve the product release checklist {#readiness}

1. Quality Unit: approve a product-and-market release checklist before processing batches under this SOP.
2. Identify the manufacturing and testing sites, applicable authorisations, release authority and qualified deputies.
3. Regulatory Affairs: identify approved specifications, methods, commitments and required market-specific release steps.
4. Include import-testing and certification requirements or justified authorised exemptions where applicable.
5. Define the authoritative batch identifier, status system, controlled record repository and quarantine safeguards.
6. Define who may change status and how a second authorised person or validated control verifies the result.
7. Train reviewers on product risks and the checklist before independent review.

Use explicit statuses such as quarantined, under review, rejected and released for the named market. Record EU certification separately from the physical or electronic transfer to saleable stock. A certificate of analysis alone is not release authority.

### 4.2. Assemble and reconcile the batch dossier {#assemble}

1. Production Owner: submit the controlled batch record with all executed pages, attachments and approved master-record identity.
2. Reconcile batch, material, equipment, processing, packaging and sample identifiers across the dossier.
3. Include reviewed laboratory results, deviations, OOS/OOT events and relevant changes.
4. Include applicable environmental, utility, cleaning, calibration and validation status evidence.
5. Include packaging specimens, label reconciliation, yield checks and traceability records.
6. Identify required supplier, contract-manufacturer and contract-laboratory evidence.
7. Quality Reviewer: log missing, conflicting or late records as queries with owners.
8. Keep the batch on hold while required evidence is unavailable.

Use original electronic records and relevant audit trails where these are the authoritative record. A printout must not conceal failed tests, omitted runs or later edits.

### 4.3. Review manufacture, testing and compliance {#review}

1. Quality Reviewer: compare executed work with approved procedures and the product release checklist.
2. Verify completion, sequence, required signatures, dates, calculations, yields and reconciliation against defined limits.
3. Confirm materials, equipment and systems were authorised for use when the batch was processed.
4. Examine specification compliance and laboratory-review status, including original suspect results and their disposition.
5. Review deviations and changes for batch, related-product, validation and authorisation impact.
6. Verify required actions affecting this batch were completed before release.
7. Assess relevant stability signals, complaints and trends that could challenge batch quality.
8. Record the evidence reviewed and each query's resolution.

Do not silently correct source records during review. Return queries to the accountable record owner for attributable corrections under Good Documentation Practices. Unexplained discrepancies require investigation even if final tests pass.

### 4.4. Decide release, hold or rejection {#disposition}

1. Quality Unit: confirm all required evidence is available and the batch meets applicable release criteria.
2. Record the batch, product, market, quantity, decision, reason, approver and decision time.
3. Reject a batch that fails applicable specifications or quality criteria.
4. Keep the batch on hold when evidence is insufficient for a justified decision.
5. If CAPA remains open, document why pending systemic work does not leave this batch's quality or release requirements unresolved.
6. Complete every batch-specific prerequisite before release.
7. For EU supply, QP: perform and record certification within the applicable authorisation before sale or supply.
8. Communicate formal certification or release authority to the site responsible for saleable-stock transfer.

An unexpected deviation is not a general exception to registered specifications. Use Annex 16 section 3 only after QP and Regulatory assessment confirms its applicability and all conditions. This reference supplies no routine deviation-based release route.

Commercial urgency or product shortage does not authorise bypassing a required release criterion.

### 4.5. Verify the release reaches distribution {#transfer}

1. Distribution Owner: authenticate the release instruction and match batch, market, quantity and expiry.
2. Change only the authorised stock to saleable status.
3. Verify the resulting warehouse, enterprise and external-distributor status using the approved independent or validated check.
4. Record who changed status, when, and the verification evidence.
5. If an interface fails or statuses conflict, block shipment and reconcile the discrepancy.
6. Do not repeat a release transaction until the actual downstream state is known.
7. Retain traceability from the distribution record to the release decision.

Where movement under quarantine is permitted, maintain quarantine safeguards and the required written arrangements. Movement under quarantine is not saleable release.

### 4.6. Handle outages and evidence discovered after release {#exceptions}

1. If the release system is unavailable, maintain quarantine unless an approved continuity procedure preserves all release and stock-control safeguards.
2. Use a controlled, attributable release record; never infer authorisation from an informal message.
3. Reconcile manual decisions and system status before normal shipment resumes.
4. If new evidence challenges a released batch, notify Quality and Regulatory Affairs immediately.
5. Block further distribution and start the quality-defect/recall assessment.
6. Identify every consignee already supplied using the distribution records.
7. Record the original release, new evidence, reassessment and protective actions without erasing the original decision.
8. If an authorised approver is unavailable, use a qualified appointed deputy or keep the batch on hold.

### 4.7. Retain release evidence and monitor failures {#records}

Quality Unit: retain the release checklist, reviewed dossier, query log, investigations, decision, certification where applicable, stock-transfer verification and distribution links.

Document Control: assign retention from applicable law, product expiry, authorisation commitments and legal holds. Preserve accessible original electronic evidence and required metadata, not only a release summary.

Quality Unit: define a review frequency based on batch volume and product risk. Monitor incomplete dossiers, repeated queries, overdue reviews, status mismatches and premature-release events. Escalate any unauthorised release immediately through Deviation Handling. Route repeated record or interface failures to CAPA and Management Review.

At retrospective review, confirm that the evidence supporting release existed at the time of the decision. Later completion of a missing prerequisite does not make an earlier unauthorised release acceptable.

## 5. Adoption and closure checklist {#checklist}

- Confirm: Product/market-specific evidence and authority are defined before review.
- Confirm: The dossier is complete and identities agree across real source records.
- Confirm: Review tests compliance and evidence completeness rather than relying on final test pass alone.
- Confirm: Only the authorised role makes an evidence-supported decision; certification is distinct from stock transfer.
- Confirm: Authorised release is accurately reflected at every relevant distribution boundary.
- Confirm: Urgency and recovery retain release authority and enable recall assessment after new evidence.
- Confirm: Release can be reconstructed at decision time, and premature release is visible and escalated.
