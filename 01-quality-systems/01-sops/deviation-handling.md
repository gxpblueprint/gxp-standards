# 01-SOP-DEV: Deviation Handling

> **Use this SOP to:** report, contain, investigate, disposition, correct, and close unplanned GMP departures.

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines how to manage unplanned departures from approved procedures, specifications, validated states, regulatory commitments, or expected GMP conditions.

The deviation process must:

- reported promptly
- assessed for product, process, patient, and data integrity impact
- contained before further use or release where needed
- investigated with depth matched to product, patient, data integrity, and recurrence risk
- linked to CAPA and change control where required
- closed with Quality approval and effectiveness checks when risk requires them

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to deviations in manufacturing, packaging, warehousing, QC laboratories, QA, engineering, maintenance, calibration, cleaning, validation, computerized systems, suppliers, outsourced activities, and documentation.

Examples include:

- missed, late, or incorrect GMP step
- OOS, OOT, atypical result, or unexplained discrepancy
- equipment, utility, environmental, cleaning, calibration, or system failure
- wrong material, label, document version, recipe, method, or specification
- data integrity concern, missing record, uncontrolled correction, audit trail anomaly, or shared-account use
- unauthorized change or temporary workaround
- supplier or outsourced activity failure affecting GMP material, service, data, or record

## 4. Definitions

- **Deviation:** An unplanned departure from an approved instruction, specification, process, validated state, regulatory commitment, or expected GMP condition.
- **Event:** An observation that may become a deviation after triage.
- **Containment:** Immediate action to prevent further impact while the issue is assessed.
- **Correction:** Action to fix the immediate problem.
- **Root cause:** The most specific, evidence-supported cause that, if removed or controlled, prevents recurrence.
- **CAPA:** Corrective and preventive action to address root cause or systemic risk.
- **Impact assessment:** Documented evaluation of actual or potential effect on product quality, patient safety, data integrity, regulatory commitments, and validated state.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| All personnel | Report suspected deviations immediately and preserve affected records, materials, and conditions. |
| Area Owner | Initiates the record, applies containment, supports investigation, and owns local corrections. |
| Quality Unit | Classifies the deviation, approves containment/disposition, reviews investigation adequacy, approves CAPA and closure. |
| Investigator | Leads fact gathering, impact assessment, root cause analysis, and investigation report. |
| SMEs | Provide technical, validation, laboratory, engineering, supplier, computerized system, regulatory, or data integrity input. |
| CAPA Owner | Implements assigned corrective/preventive actions and provides evidence. |
| Qualified Person / Batch Release Owner, where applicable | Confirms batch release or certification impact is assessed before release. |

## 6. Classification

| Class | Use when | Minimum expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Actual or likely impact to patient safety, released product, sterility assurance, identity/strength/purity, batch release decision, regulatory commitment, or critical data integrity. | Immediate Quality notification, product/system hold, full investigation, root cause, CAPA, Quality disposition, effectiveness check. |
| Major | Potential GMP or product quality impact that is contained before release or use. | Documented investigation, impact assessment, root cause or justified probable cause, CAPA where needed, Quality closure. |
| Minor | Limited GMP departure with no product quality, data integrity, validated state, or regulatory impact after assessment. | Documented rationale, correction, trendable closure. |
| Recurrent | Similar event repeats or trend shows process weakness. | Upgrade classification where needed; investigate systemic cause and CAPA. |

Quality may upgrade or downgrade classification with documented rationale.

## 6.1 Scenario Rules

| Scenario | Required action |
| --- | --- |
| Possible released-product impact | Notify Quality immediately, open deviation, start product impact assessment, and escalate to management / Regulatory / QP where required. |
| Batch, sample, material, record, or system still under site control | Place affected item on hold or equivalent control until Quality disposition. |
| Minor documentation error with no quality impact | Correct per GDP, document rationale, and trend if repeated. |
| Audit trail, raw data, or original record is missing or altered | Preserve available records, restrict further changes, notify Quality, and investigate as a data integrity event. |
| Same issue repeats | Treat as recurrent; assess systemic cause and CAPA need. |

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Report and preserve

1. Stop the affected activity if continuing could increase risk.
2. Notify the supervisor and Quality immediately for critical or potentially batch-impacting events.
3. Preserve physical evidence, electronic records, audit trails, printouts, samples, labels, logs, and system state.
4. Do not alter, recreate, overwrite, delete, or "clean up" records before Quality assessment.
5. Open a deviation record within the site-defined timeline, usually the same business day.

### 7.2 Contain

Containment must protect product, process, and records while facts are gathered.

Containment may include:

- quarantine or hold affected batch, material, label, component, sample, equipment, room, utility, or record
- restrict system access or disable affected function
- stop shipment, testing, processing, or release
- preserve backup, audit trail, instrument file, or raw data
- segregate obsolete or incorrect documents/forms
- notify supplier or contract acceptor
- open a linked incident, OOS/OOT, complaint, or change control where applicable

Quality must approve product or record disposition when GMP impact is possible.

### 7.3 Initial assessment

The initial assessment must answer:

- What happened, when, where, and who discovered it?
- What approved requirement, specification, instruction, or expected condition was not met?
- What product, batch, material, sample, process, document, system, equipment, data, or supplier is affected?
- Is there immediate risk to patient safety, product quality, data integrity, validated state, or regulatory commitments?
- Is any product, material, system, or record already released or used?
- What containment is in place?
- Does the event require escalation to senior management, Regulatory Affairs, QP, supplier, or customer?

### 7.4 Investigation planning

Investigation depth must be proportionate to risk, complexity, recurrence, and uncertainty.

The investigation plan should define:

- investigation owner and SMEs
- facts and records to collect
- batches, lots, time period, systems, equipment, methods, or suppliers in scope
- interviews required
- data and audit trails to review
- laboratory or engineering checks needed
- similar historical events or trends
- expected due date and interim controls

Do not close an investigation by assuming operator error without evidence and without assessing system, procedure, training, equipment, environment, workload, and supervision factors.

### 7.5 Root cause analysis

Use an RCA method suitable to the event, such as 5 Whys, fishbone, fault tree, process mapping, or human-factors review.

Root cause conclusions must be:

- evidence-based
- specific enough to drive action
- linked to the observed facts
- checked against recurrence history
- reviewed for whether a broader system weakness exists

If root cause cannot be determined, document the most probable cause, uncertainty, additional controls, and monitoring plan.

### 7.6 Impact assessment

Assess actual and potential impact before disposition or closure.

| Area | Minimum questions |
| --- | --- |
| Product / batch | Could identity, strength, purity, safety, quality, efficacy, sterility, contamination control, or stability be affected? |
| Records / data | Are records complete, attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original/true copy, accurate, consistent, enduring, and available? |
| Validated state | Is equipment, method, process, cleaning, utility, computerized system, or transport validation affected? |
| Scope | Are other batches, lots, products, samples, systems, documents, suppliers, or time periods affected? |
| Regulatory | Does this affect a filing, commitment, reportable event, market action, or customer notification? |
| Release | Can the affected product, material, data, or record be used or released? What evidence supports the decision? |

### 7.7 CAPA and linked actions

CAPA is required when:

- root cause indicates systemic weakness
- event is critical or major with recurrence risk
- trend indicates repeat failures
- procedure, training, design, supplier, process, system, or validation controls are inadequate
- regulatory, audit, or management review commitments require action

Use change control for planned changes to approved procedures, validated systems, specifications, equipment, suppliers, materials, or processes.

### 7.8 Closure

Quality may approve closure when:

- event description is clear
- classification is justified
- containment and product disposition are complete
- investigation scope is adequate
- impact assessment is complete
- root cause or probable cause is justified
- CAPA/change controls are opened or completed as required
- effectiveness check is defined where needed
- records are complete and GDP-compliant

Critical and major deviations should not be closed only with "retrain operator" unless the investigation proves training was the root cause and the training action is designed to prevent recurrence.

## 8. Timelines

Suggested default timelines:

- notification to Quality: immediately for critical/major, same business day for minor
- deviation record opened: same business day or within 1 working day
- initial assessment and containment: within 1-3 working days
- investigation completion: within 30 calendar days unless extension is approved
- extension: approved by Quality with reason, interim controls, and new due date

## 9. Records

Deviation records must include:

- unique ID, title, date/time, area, owner, classification
- event description and requirement not met
- immediate containment
- affected product/material/system/record scope
- investigation plan and evidence reviewed
- root cause analysis
- impact assessment and disposition
- correction, CAPA, change control, and effectiveness check references
- Quality approvals and closure date

## 10. Metrics and Trending

Quality should trend:

- deviations by area, process, product, system, supplier, root cause, classification, and recurrence
- overdue investigations and extensions
- repeat deviations after CAPA
- data integrity events
- human error classifications and underlying system causes
- batch release delays due to deviations

Trends should feed CAPA, management review, internal audit planning, supplier review, validation periodic review, and training updates.

## 11. Quick Checklist

Before closure, confirm:

- Affected items were contained and dispositioned.
- Original records and electronic data were preserved.
- Scope includes related batches, time periods, systems, and suppliers.
- Root cause is evidence-based.
- Product quality and data integrity impact are clear.
- CAPA/change controls are assigned where required.
- Effectiveness check is defined for significant or recurring issues.
