# 01-SOP-OOS: OOS, OOT and Atypical Laboratory Result Investigations

> **Use this SOP to:** preserve suspect results, investigate their cause and support a scientifically justified Quality disposition.

:::caution Draft reference — adapt before use
This is a reference draft, not an approved site procedure. Quality and the Process Owner must confirm applicability, roles, records and operating arrangements before adoption. Complete a qualified user walkthrough and approval before operational use.
:::

## 1. Purpose and scope {#scope}

Out-of-specification (OOS) means a result outside the acceptance criteria established in an approved specification.

Chemistry-based GMP laboratory results for human finished pharmaceuticals, associated materials, in-process tests and stability programmes. OOT means a result inconsistent with an approved trend rule; an atypical result warrants assessment even when no rule exists. Method-specific biological assays, sterility testing, environmental monitoring and real-time release need specialist procedures. This SOP does not replace them.

The instructions below are proposed GxP Blueprint controls for adoption. Regulatory requirements apply only within their legal scope. Guidance informs the design; it does not make every implementation choice a legal obligation. Sources were checked on 8 September 2026. Veterinary, investigational and specialist product requirements need separate assessment.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Analyst | Preserves data and preparations, reports the result and supplies contemporaneous observations. |
| Laboratory Supervisor | Leads the initial laboratory assessment and checks scientific justification. |
| Investigator and technical specialists | Assess the full process, sampling and related-batch impact. |
| Quality Unit | Approves investigation plans, additional testing, invalidation and final disposition. |
| Contract Laboratory | Provides all data and findings to the contracting manufacturer's Quality Unit. |
| Regulatory Affairs / Qualified Person | Assesses market reporting, authorisation and certification implications within their authority. |

Quality Unit means the adopting organisation's authorised quality function; US quality control unit responsibilities remain with that function. Appoint qualified deputies with documented authority. One person may hold compatible roles; preserve the independent checks required below.

## 4. Procedure {#procedure}

### 4.1. Define result rules before testing {#readiness}

1. Laboratory Supervisor: verify Quality Unit approval of the controlled method, sampling plan, specification and reportable-result calculation before testing. Retain the approved versions and approval identity. If approval is missing, stop testing and obtain Quality review. A supervisor acting as Quality approver must hold documented Quality Unit authority.
2. Define authorised replicate calculations, variability limits and any staged compendial tests in the method.
3. Establish product-specific OOT rules and alert ownership using process, method and stability knowledge.
4. Define initial notification, investigation and escalation times according to patient, product and data risk.
5. Assign qualified deputies and a controlled outage record.
6. Define who can approve hypothesis testing, retesting and resampling.

Do not change a specification, rounding rule or reportable-result definition after seeing an unfavourable result. An approved staged test is not permission for unlimited additional testing.

### 4.2. Report, preserve and contain {#preserve}

1. Analyst: stop unapproved repeat testing when an OOS, OOT or atypical result is identified.
2. Inform the Laboratory Supervisor and Quality through the defined route.
3. Preserve original data, metadata, audit trails, calculations, chromatograms and instrument conditions.
4. Retain samples, standards, solutions and preparations within their stability and safe-storage limits.
5. Record preparation expiry or unavoidable loss; do not conceal it by remaking preparations.
6. Quality Unit: identify affected lots and apply holds while impact is assessed.
7. For distributed or stability lots, notify Regulatory Affairs immediately for reporting and market-action assessment.
8. Open the investigation record and identify all linked sample and batch records.

Do not discard a result because a supervisor expects the batch to pass. If potential integrity issues exist, preserve access and system evidence through Data Integrity.

### 4.3. Perform the initial laboratory investigation {#laboratory}

1. Laboratory Supervisor: review the analyst's account with the contemporaneous records.
2. Check sample identity, preparation, dilutions, standards, reagents, calculations and method version.
3. Examine instrument status, suitability, sequence, integrations, audit trails and environmental conditions.
4. Record the proposed cause and evidence that would confirm or refute it.
5. Before hypothesis testing, obtain the required approval for the question, method, preparations, data treatment and stop point.
6. Keep investigational testing distinct from reportable release testing.
7. Quality Unit: invalidate a result only when documented evidence establishes a causative test error.
8. Retain the original result, reason, evidence and approval in the permanent record.

A passing reinjection, retest or statistical outlier designation alone does not establish a laboratory error. If no causative laboratory error is demonstrated, continue to the full investigation.

### 4.4. Expand the investigation and control additional testing {#full-investigation}

1. Quality Unit: appoint a full-investigation owner when the laboratory phase does not establish a causative error.
2. Review manufacturing, sampling, storage, process variation, materials, equipment and related batches or products.
3. Assess similar historical results, including invalidated results and contract-laboratory events.
4. Before additional testing, approve the scientific rationale, sample source, number of tests, analysts, acceptance interpretation and stop point.
5. Use retesting for the original sample and resampling only when the sampling rationale is justified.
6. If the original sampling method is inadequate, approve a scientifically sound replacement before resampling.
7. Record every test and any deviation from the approved plan.
8. Do not expand the test count because the planned results are unfavourable.

Do not average initial OOS results with later results to obtain a passing value. Preserve approved method-defined replicate calculations. Do not use an outlier test to invalidate a chemical OOS result.

Contract Laboratory: send the complete investigation and all relevant results to the manufacturer's Quality Unit. A contract certificate does not transfer the manufacturer's disposition responsibility.

### 4.5. Evaluate all evidence and decide disposition {#disposition}

1. Quality Unit: evaluate the original result, investigation, additional results and remaining uncertainty together.
2. Record whether the result is invalidated with cause, confirmed as product failure, or unresolved after investigation.
3. Reject product that fails established specifications and applicable quality criteria.
4. If unresolved, retain the OOS in the assessment; do not convert uncertainty into a passing result.
5. Maintain hold unless a full investigation supports a documented, scientifically justified disposition under the applicable release procedure.
6. Assess related batches and products even when the original batch is rejected.
7. For OOT or atypical results, assess process drift and ability to meet specifications through shelf life.
8. Link CAPA and any required change control.
9. Obtain QP and Regulatory Affairs assessment where applicable before the release decision.

This procedure supplies no automatic release exception for an unexplained OOS. Additional passing tests alone are insufficient. Continue the reporting assessment for distributed product while investigation proceeds.

### 4.6. Manage delay, absence and system loss {#exceptions}

1. If delay threatens sample stability, Laboratory Supervisor: preserve preparations and escalate the decision before expiry where possible.
2. Do not perform unauthorised testing because an approver is absent.
3. Use a qualified delegated approver with recorded authority.
4. During system loss, preserve electronic evidence and use controlled numbered paper records for permitted activities.
5. After restoration, reconcile sample IDs, sequence numbers, tests and entries before using the record for disposition.
6. Before extending a due date, Quality Unit: reassess product holds, evidence loss and reporting deadlines.
7. Record overdue work without backdating approval.
8. If verification fails or new evidence contradicts closure, reopen the investigation and reassess released lots.

An investigation extension does not extend a regulatory reporting deadline. Use the complaint/quality-defect process for reporting and market-action decisions.

### 4.7. Close the investigation and trend results {#records}

Quality Unit: approve closure only after the report contains:

- Sample, product, batch, method and specification identities.
- Original result, detection time, raw-data locations and preservation actions.
- Laboratory review, tested hypotheses and any invalidation rationale.
- Approved additional-testing plans and every resulting value.
- Process and related-batch assessment, disposition and reporting links.
- Cause or uncertainty, CAPA, approvals and closure date.

Laboratory Supervisor: trend OOS, OOT, atypical results, invalidation reasons, repeat tests, laboratory causes and overdue investigations. Define the review frequency and escalation thresholds with Quality based on test volume and risk. Use consistent denominators and include contract-laboratory results. Escalate emerging product or data concerns immediately.

Retain all data under Document Control and applicable laboratory/batch retention requirements. A reviewer must be able to distinguish the original result from investigational and replacement results.

## 5. Adoption and closure checklist {#checklist}

- Confirm: Predefined methods, results, trend triggers and authorities prevent retrospective rules.
- Confirm: Original evidence is retained and affected lots are contained before further decisions.
- Confirm: Laboratory invalidation depends on demonstrated causation, not the desirability of replacement data.
- Confirm: A full investigation considers process causes; additional testing has prospective scientific limits.
- Confirm: All results and uncertainty reach authorised disposition; rejected lots still prompt wider investigation.
- Confirm: Degraded operation preserves original evidence, authority and independent reporting clocks.
- Confirm: Complete result history remains reviewable and repeated invalidations are visible in trending.
