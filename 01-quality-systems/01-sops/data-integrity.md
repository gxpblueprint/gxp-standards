# 01-SOP-DI: Data Integrity Governance

> **Use this SOP to:** create, review, protect, investigate, retain, and retrieve trustworthy GMP data.

---

## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines data integrity expectations for paper, electronic, and hybrid GMP records.

It ensures that GMP decisions are based on trustworthy records and that data integrity risks are prevented, detected, investigated, corrected, and trended.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all GMP data generated, processed, reviewed, reported, retained, archived, retrieved, transmitted, migrated, or destroyed by personnel, suppliers, systems, instruments, equipment, spreadsheets, and outsourced activities.

Includes:

- batch and packaging records
- laboratory raw data and metadata
- equipment, calibration, maintenance, environmental, cleaning, warehouse, and utility records
- deviations, CAPA, change controls, complaints, training, supplier, and audit records
- computerized system audit trails, electronic signatures, reports, backups, archives, and interfaces
- paper records, electronic records, and hybrid records

## 4. Principles

GMP data must be:

- **Attributable:** Linked to the person, system, instrument, or process that created or changed it.
- **Legible:** Readable and understandable for the retention period.
- **Contemporaneous:** Recorded at the time the activity occurs.
- **Original / true copy:** Original record or verified copy preserving meaning and metadata.
- **Accurate:** Correct, complete, and free from unjustified alteration.
- **Complete:** All data, including repeats, reprocessing, audit trails, and metadata, are retained where needed to reconstruct the activity.
- **Consistent:** Chronological, controlled, and aligned across records.
- **Enduring:** Durable and protected from loss, damage, or unauthorized change.
- **Available:** Retrievable for review, investigation, and inspection throughout retention.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Senior Management | Sets data integrity expectations, provides resources, and supports open reporting of concerns. |
| Quality Unit | Owns DI governance, approves controls, reviews critical events, trends DI metrics, and escalates systemic risk. |
| Data Owner | Defines record purpose, criticality, retention, review, access, archive, and disposition requirements. |
| System Owner | Maintains validated state, access control, audit trails, backup/restore, and periodic review for computerized systems. |
| Process Owner | Ensures data generated in the process are complete, reviewed, and protected. |
| All Personnel | Follow GDP, use unique credentials, report errors honestly, and preserve original data. |
| Suppliers / Contract Acceptors | Maintain DI controls for outsourced GMP data and make records available as agreed. |

## 6. Data Lifecycle Controls

| Lifecycle stage | Required controls |
| --- | --- |
| Create / acquire | Approved method, trained personnel, controlled forms/templates, qualified instruments/systems, unique users. |
| Process / calculate | Approved formulas, locked templates, validated calculations, review of changes and reprocessing. |
| Review / approve | Independent review where required, audit trail review for critical records, documented approval. |
| Report / transfer | Controlled reports, complete context, interface checks, no selective reporting. |
| Retain / archive | Protected storage, metadata preservation, backup, readability, retrieval testing. |
| Destroy / dispose | Approved retention met, Quality approval where required, documented destruction, secure disposal. |

## 6.1 Data Integrity Scenario Rules

| Scenario | Required action |
| --- | --- |
| Original data are missing | Stop relying on the record for GMP decisions, notify Quality, preserve remaining evidence, and open a deviation. |
| Audit trail shows unexplained GMP data changes | Hold the affected record or decision, review the change with the data owner, and investigate before release or approval. |
| Shared credentials used for GMP work | Stop use, preserve audit trail/system evidence, assess affected records, and open a data integrity deviation. |
| Paper and electronic records conflict | Identify the authoritative record, place the decision on hold, reconcile with Quality approval. |
| Uncontrolled spreadsheet used for GMP calculation | Verify calculation independently, assess affected decisions, and move the tool into controlled use or retire it. |

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Record creation

1. Use only approved records, forms, systems, methods, templates, and instruments.
2. Record data at the time of activity.
3. Use unique user accounts for electronic records and approved signature/initial controls for paper records.
4. Do not use uncontrolled scratch notes, unofficial spreadsheets, shared accounts, or personal storage for GMP data.
5. If temporary notes are unavoidable, attach or transcribe them under GDP controls and preserve them as part of the record where they contain original GMP data.

### 7.2 Original data and metadata

Original data include raw measurements, instrument files, audit trails, integration parameters, reprocessing history, calculations, approvals, and metadata needed to reconstruct the activity.

Metadata may include:

- date/time
- user ID
- instrument/system ID
- method/version
- sample/material/batch ID
- units
- processing parameters
- audit trail events
- review status

Original data and metadata must remain linked and protected.

### 7.3 Corrections and changes

Corrections must:

- preserve the original entry
- identify who made the correction and when
- explain why when the reason is not obvious
- be reviewed where required

Electronic systems must use audit trails for GMP-relevant changes. Paper corrections must follow the GDP SOP.

### 7.4 Review and audit trails

Record reviewers must confirm:

- record completeness
- correct calculations and transcription
- required signatures and dates
- linked deviations or investigations
- audit trail entries relevant to GMP data changes
- original data and metadata are available

Audit trails that capture changes to GMP data must be reviewed with the related GMP record before batch release, sample approval, or quality decision where relevant.

### 7.5 Access control

Systems must use:

- unique user IDs
- role-based access
- no shared accounts for GMP actions
- timely access removal for leavers and role changes
- periodic access review
- controlled privileged access
- supplier/remote access approval and monitoring

Read-only shared viewing accounts may be allowed only when they cannot create, modify, delete, approve, or affect GMP records or configuration.

### 7.6 Hybrid records

For hybrid paper/electronic records, define:

- authoritative record
- which components are paper versus electronic
- how metadata and audit trails are reviewed
- how printouts are controlled
- how electronic source data are retained and retrieved
- how discrepancies between paper and electronic data are resolved

Printed records must not replace dynamic electronic records unless a true-copy process preserves required meaning and metadata.

### 7.7 Spreadsheets and end-user tools

GMP-impacting spreadsheets, macros, scripts, and reports must be controlled by intended use and risk.

Controls include:

- approved template and version
- locked formulas and protected cells
- independent formula verification
- test cases for critical calculations
- controlled storage and access
- change history
- periodic review
- retirement of obsolete versions

### 7.8 Backup, archive, and retrieval

Backup and archive controls must ensure:

- data and metadata are protected
- backups are monitored
- restore is tested based on risk
- archive remains readable
- records are retrievable during retention
- disposal is approved and documented

### 7.9 Data integrity events

Potential DI events include:

- missing original data
- backdating or undocumented late entry
- shared credentials for GMP work
- audit trail disabled or not reviewed
- deletion or overwriting of records
- selective reporting
- unexplained repeated testing
- uncontrolled spreadsheet or report
- mismatched paper/electronic records
- unauthorized data change

Report suspected DI events immediately as deviations. Quality must assess product impact, data reliability, need for expanded review, and CAPA.

## 8. Governance and Periodic Review

Quality should maintain a DI governance review covering:

- DI deviations and trends
- audit trail review performance
- access review findings
- backup/restore status
- spreadsheet inventory
- DI training completion
- supplier/outsourced data issues
- computerized system periodic review outputs
- inspection or audit observations

## 9. Records

Maintain:

- DI risk assessments
- audit trail review records
- access review records
- data flow maps for critical systems/processes
- spreadsheet/register controls
- backup/restore evidence
- DI deviations and CAPA
- DI training
- supplier DI assessments

## 10. Quick Checklist

- Can the activity be reconstructed from original data and metadata?
- Are all changes attributable and justified?
- Are audit trails reviewed where they affect GMP decisions?
- Are records protected through retention?
- Are electronic and paper components linked?
- Are DI concerns reported without delay or blame?
