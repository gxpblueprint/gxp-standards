# 01-SOP-PQR: Product Quality Review and Annual Product Review

> **Use this SOP to:** evaluate product and process performance over a defined period and turn adverse trends into owned quality actions.

:::caution Draft reference — adapt before use
This is a reference draft, not an approved site procedure. Quality and the Process Owner must confirm applicability, roles, records and operating arrangements before adoption. Complete a qualified user walkthrough and approval before operational use.
:::

## 1. Purpose and scope {#scope}

Periodic quality evaluation of human finished medicinal products, including relevant export products and outsourced manufacture or testing. The review supports ongoing quality oversight; it does not replace batch release, stability investigation, complaint handling or real-time risk action.

The instructions below are proposed GxP Blueprint controls for adoption. Regulatory requirements apply only within their legal scope. Guidance informs the design; it does not make every implementation choice a legal obligation. Sources were checked on 8 September 2026. Veterinary, investigational and specialist product requirements need separate assessment.

## 2. References and process boundaries {#references}

- [US FDA — 21 CFR Part 211; eCFR displayed current through 2026-09-03](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211): §211.180(e)(1)–(2). At-least-annual evaluation for each drug product, representative approved/rejected batch review and specified event records.
- [European Commission — EU GMP Chapter 1; operational 31 January 2013](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/e458c423-f564-4171-b344-030a461c567f_en?filename=vol4-chap1_2013-01_en.pdf): §§1.10(i)–(xii), 1.11. EU product review dataset, normal annual cadence, justified grouping and manufacturer/authorisation-holder responsibilities.

Related procedures retain their decision authority:

- [Capa Management](./capa-management.md): Owned actions and effectiveness assessment.
- [Change Control](./change-control.md): Changes to processes, controls or specifications.
- [Oos Oot Investigations](./oos-oot-investigations.md): Investigation of laboratory signals.
- [Supplier Qualification](./supplier-qualification.md): Contracted data access and oversight.
- [Complaints Quality Defects And Recalls](./complaints-quality-defects-and-recalls.md): Immediate action on distributed-product signals.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Review Coordinator | Maintains the product schedule, dataset register and report. |
| Data Owners | Supply complete, reconciled source data and explain gaps. |
| Product / Process Specialists | Evaluate trends, variability and scientific implications. |
| Quality Unit | Approves scope, completeness, conclusions and actions. |
| Marketing Authorisation Holder / Regulatory Affairs | Supplies dossier, commitment and market information and evaluates regulatory changes. |
| Senior Management | Provides resources and follows systemic risks through Management Review. |

Quality Unit means the adopting organisation's authorised quality function; US quality control unit responsibilities remain with that function. Appoint qualified deputies with documented authority. One person may hold compatible roles; preserve the independent checks required below.

## 4. Procedure {#procedure}

### 4.1. Approve the review programme {#readiness}

1. Review Coordinator: maintain an inventory of products, markets, manufacturers and review periods.
2. Quality Unit: approve an annual schedule with data cut-off, delivery, review and approval dates.
3. For US products, ensure evaluation occurs at least annually for each drug product.
4. For EU products, use the normal annual review expectation; document any justified rolling arrangement and its coverage.
5. Define manufacturer and authorisation-holder responsibilities in written agreements when they differ.
6. Assign data owners, qualified reviewers, deputies and escalation contacts.
7. Record the controlled report template, storage and version history.

A combined EU/US review may be used only when it preserves each applicable requirement. Management Review is not a substitute for the product-specific evaluation.

### 4.2. Define scope and reconcile the batch population {#population}

1. Review Coordinator: identify the product, strengths, presentations, markets, sites and review period.
2. Explain any grouping scientifically and retain visibility of each product's distinct risks and results.
3. Reconcile the population against manufacturing, laboratory, release and distribution registers.
4. Identify approved, rejected, reprocessed, aborted and still-open batches without duplicate counting.
5. For US representative-batch review, document the selection rationale and include both approved and rejected outcomes.
6. For the EU review, include all failed batches and the other required review topics.
7. Reconcile late events affecting earlier periods and carry forward previous actions.
8. If no batches were manufactured, review stability, complaints, changes, commitments and prior actions; do not omit the product automatically.

Do not combine data from different methods, units or specifications without recording the conversion and comparability assessment.

### 4.3. Collect and verify the review dataset {#data}

Data Owners: supply the following with source identifiers, extraction date, period and reconciliation evidence. Record non-applicability with a reason and Quality approval.

| Dataset | Required review content |
| --- | --- |
| Materials and supply chain | Starting and packaging materials, new sources, active-substance traceability and supplier changes. |
| Process and testing | Critical in-process controls, finished results, variability, yields and failed batches with investigations. |
| Quality events | Significant deviations, OOS/OOT results, complaints, recalls, quality returns, US salvaged-product records and investigations. |
| Action performance | CAPA effectiveness, earlier product/process/equipment actions and overdue commitments. |
| Changes and authorisations | Process/method changes; submitted, granted and refused variations; post-marketing commitments. |
| Stability | Scheduled and completed testing, adverse trends, failures and outstanding time points. |
| Equipment and utilities | Relevant qualification status and events that challenge the validated state. |
| Agreements | Current outsourced-activity responsibilities, data availability and agreement status. |

Review Coordinator: check completeness with each Data Owner. Distinguish zero events from missing data. Retain the source data or a controlled retrievable reference. Verify spreadsheet calculations and extraction filters before relying on them.

### 4.4. Evaluate trends and escalate emerging risks {#evaluate}

1. Product Specialist: compare results with specifications, prior periods and known process variability.
2. Define the statistical method and its assumptions before interpreting the result.
3. Separate specification limits from process-control or alert limits.
4. Examine trends by product, site, method, supplier or equipment where pooling could hide a problem.
5. Evaluate recurrence, adverse stability trends, repeated invalidations and ineffective actions.
6. Document small-sample limitations; absence of a statistical signal does not prove control.
7. Assess whether specifications, process controls, validation or monitoring need revision.
8. If a serious product or data concern appears, notify Quality immediately and open the linked investigation.
9. Do not wait for report approval to assess affected released batches or reporting obligations.

Quality Unit: require scientific explanation for conclusions. A graph without evaluated implications is not a completed review.

### 4.5. Approve conclusions and accountable actions {#conclusions}

1. Review Coordinator: summarise each topic's conclusion, evidence and remaining uncertainty.
2. State whether the process remains consistent and whether specifications and controls remain suitable.
3. List necessary CAPA, revalidation, change controls and further monitoring with owners and due dates.
4. Explain decisions that no action is required when adverse observations were assessed.
5. Quality Unit: review the dataset coverage and scientific rationale before approval.
6. Obtain manufacturer and authorisation-holder evaluation according to their written responsibilities.
7. Send resource needs and systemic risks to Management Review.
8. Track resulting actions through their authoritative processes; do not close them by approving the report.

A report must not claim a complete assessment while material source data are missing. Quality may approve a clearly limited interim assessment with protective actions, but the review remains open until its required scope is addressed.

### 4.6. Manage delay, new evidence and degraded operation {#exceptions}

1. Before a due date is missed, Review Coordinator: notify Quality with cause, missing inputs and recovery dates.
2. Quality Unit: assess the risk of delay and escalate resources to Senior Management.
3. Preserve applicable annual obligations; an internal extension does not waive them.
4. If a deadline is missed, record the missed obligation and assess it through Deviation Handling.
5. During system loss, use verified controlled exports or records with traceable cut-off dates.
6. After restoration, reconcile source changes and rerun affected analyses before final approval.
7. If later information changes an approved conclusion, issue a controlled addendum or revision.
8. Reopen affected actions, batch assessments and reporting decisions as required.
9. Use qualified deputies; do not replace scientific review with an administrative signature.

### 4.7. Retain the review and assess programme performance {#records}

Review Coordinator: retain the schedule, product scope, source register, reconciliations, analysis files, methods, reviewer queries, report, approvals and action links.

Quality Unit: review programme performance at a defined frequency based on product count, risk and overdue workload. Track due and completed reviews, missing datasets, late approvals, reopened conclusions and overdue actions. Escalate unresolved high-risk conclusions immediately. Confirm prior actions and their effectiveness in the next review.

Document Control: assign the retention schedule from applicable law, product lifecycle and commitments. Preserve calculation versions and data cut-off dates so a later reviewer can reproduce the conclusions.

At closure, confirm every required topic was addressed, limitations are explicit, decisions are approved and actions have owners. An approved report is evidence of review, not proof that the product cannot fail.

## 5. Adoption and closure checklist {#checklist}

- Confirm: Each product has an accountable schedule and jurisdiction-specific coverage.
- Confirm: Review populations and groupings are explicit, reconciled and scientifically defensible.
- Confirm: Each required topic has reconciled source evidence or explicit non-applicability.
- Confirm: Interpretation addresses process performance and uncertainty and triggers immediate action for serious signals.
- Confirm: Conclusions are evidence-supported and actions persist independently of report approval.
- Confirm: Delay and recovery preserve obligations and invalidate conclusions affected by new evidence.
- Confirm: The conclusions are reproducible and the review programme detects its own missed obligations.
