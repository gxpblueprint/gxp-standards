# 01-SOP-CQR: Complaints, Quality Defects and Product Recalls

> **Use this SOP to:** record complaints, investigate suspected quality defects and execute traceable product recalls when risk requires action.

:::caution Draft reference — adapt before use
This is a reference draft, not an approved site procedure. Quality and the Process Owner must confirm applicability, roles, records and operating arrangements before adoption. Complete a qualified user walkthrough and approval before operational use.
:::

## 1. Purpose and scope {#scope}

Quality complaints and suspected defects affecting human finished medicinal products, including distributed stock. Adverse-event processing remains with Pharmacovigilance. This reference does not supply country-specific reporting forms, clinical-trial unblinding, veterinary or specialist biological-product reporting procedures.

The instructions below are proposed GxP Blueprint controls for adoption. Regulatory requirements apply only within their legal scope. Guidance informs the design; it does not make every implementation choice a legal obligation. Sources were checked on 8 September 2026. Veterinary, investigational and specialist product requirements need separate assessment.

## 3. Roles and responsibilities {#roles}

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Complaint Receiver | Records the original report and promptly sends it to Quality. |
| Quality Unit | Owns risk assessment, investigation, product holds and complaint closure. |
| Recall Coordinator and deputy | Maintains readiness, executes authorised recall strategy and reconciles quantities. |
| Regulatory Affairs | Determines each market obligation, deadline and reporting route; retains submissions. |
| Marketing Authorisation Holder | Participates in defect and market-action decisions under written agreements. |
| Qualified Person, where applicable | Assesses certification impact and receives defect and recall information. |
| Pharmacovigilance | Assesses and processes safety reports under its controlled procedure. |
| Distribution Owner | Blocks supply, identifies consignees and controls returned stock. |

Quality Unit means the adopting organisation's authorised quality function; US quality control unit responsibilities remain with that function. Appoint qualified deputies with documented authority. One person may hold compatible roles; preserve the independent checks required below.

## 4. Procedure {#procedure}

### 4.1. Maintain recall and reporting readiness {#readiness}

1. Quality Unit: approve named decision-makers, deputies and an out-of-hours contact route before using this SOP.
2. Recall Coordinator: maintain market, authority, distributor, contract-site and authorisation-holder contact details.
3. Regulatory Affairs: approve a market-specific reporting matrix with triggers, clock-start rules, deadlines, submission routes and backup contacts.
4. Include quality-defect notifications, applicable field alerts, safety referrals and product-specific obligations.
5. Define who can initiate immediate protective action when normal signatories cannot be reached.
6. Verify access to distribution records, complaint records and controlled communication templates during system outages.
7. Define exercise frequency from product risk, distribution complexity and previous failures.

The reporting matrix is a prerequisite, not an optional later activity. A single global recall deadline is not an acceptable substitute. Test contact and traceability arrangements after material supply-chain changes.

### 4.2. Receive and triage the complaint {#intake}

1. Complaint Receiver: record oral, written, electronic and third-party reports on receipt.
2. Capture product, strength, dosage form, batch, market, description, discovery time and contact details where known.
3. Preserve the original message, photographs and supplied evidence.
4. Send suspected quality or patient-safety issues to Quality immediately through the escalation route.
5. Refer possible adverse events to Pharmacovigilance without waiting for the quality investigation.
6. Quality Unit: assess severity, possible falsification, affected population, distribution status and uncertainty.
7. Assign an investigator, risk-based due date and interim controls.

Missing batch details or an unavailable sample must not delay initial risk assessment. Record attempts to obtain the missing information. For a non-quality complaint, retain the triage reason and referral. For a decision not to investigate, retain Quality's documented reason and the responsible person's identity.

### 4.3. Investigate and contain the defect {#investigation}

1. Quality Unit: place potentially affected site stock on hold and instruct Distribution to block shipment.
2. Investigator: request samples when safe and useful, with identity, receipt condition and chain of custody.
3. Review manufacturing, testing, packaging, storage, transport, prior complaints and release records.
4. Assess related batches, products, suppliers, sites and time periods.
5. Determine whether released product may be affected before awaiting the final root cause.
6. Link the investigation to Deviation Handling and laboratory investigations where needed.
7. Record the risk assessment, causal evidence, uncertainty and review time for each interim decision.
8. Quality Unit: inform the authorisation holder, affected QP and contracted parties using the approved agreements.

A rejected batch or expired product can still require investigation and reporting assessment. Do not close the concern because stock is no longer saleable.

### 4.4. Decide market action and reporting {#market-decision}

1. Quality Unit: convene Regulatory Affairs, the authorisation holder, Recall Coordinator and QP where applicable.
2. Assess defect severity, likelihood of harm, exposed population, distribution extent and consequences of shortage.
3. Record recall, other protective action or no-recall rationale with named decision authority and review triggers.
4. Regulatory Affairs: determine notifications from the approved matrix when the trigger is recognised.
5. Record awareness time, due time, submissions, acknowledgements and follow-up commitments.
6. Reassess obligations as new information arrives.

For EU recalls, notify concerned competent authorities before action wherever possible. If a very serious risk requires immediate action, act to protect patients and notify authorities promptly. Record the reason and attempts to coordinate.

For US NDA/ANDA products, assess field alert obligations separately from recall decisions. The applicable rule includes a three-working-day reporting period for specified information. Do not wait for investigation completion. Regulatory Affairs must apply the confirmed clock and reporting route.

Commercial approval must not delay protective action. This SOP does not allow a recall to be relabelled as a routine return to avoid its controls.

### 4.5. Execute the recall {#recall-execution}

1. Recall Coordinator: record affected products, lots, markets, recall depth, target recipients and communication schedule.
2. Agree the strategy with authorities as required by the applicable market arrangements.
3. Distribution Owner: verify shipment blocks across warehouses and contracted distributors.
4. Prepare a trace list from actual shipments, including exports, samples and further distribution where available.
5. Send controlled notices identifying the product, risk, stop-use or stop-supply action, return route and response method.
6. Retain the exact notice version, recipient, dispatch time and delivery evidence.
7. Track acknowledgements and chase nonresponders using the approved escalation schedule.
8. Escalate unreachable recipients or continued supply to Quality and Regulatory Affairs.
9. Segregate and identify returned stock; prevent its re-entry to saleable inventory.

Protect patient information in communications. Pharmacovigilance and clinical contacts own patient advice within their scope. Recall staff must use the authorised communication, not invent clinical advice.

### 4.6. Verify effectiveness and close each process {#closure}

1. Recall Coordinator: reconcile quantities supplied, returned, destroyed, confirmed held, consumed and unaccounted for.
2. Avoid double-counting quantities that move between these categories.
3. Investigate discrepancies and retain the calculation basis.
4. Verify that recipients received and followed the notice; return quantity alone does not prove effectiveness.
5. Quality Unit: assess unresolved exposure and approve further action where recovery is incomplete.
6. Regulatory Affairs: obtain or document authority closure or termination requirements for each market.
For a US recall overseen by FDA, distinguish internal completion from FDA termination. Retain the FDA termination communication before recording the recall as terminated.

7. Quality Unit: approve the recall report only after reconciliation, effectiveness, disposition and required authority interactions are complete.
8. Close the complaint separately after investigation, response and follow-up are documented.
9. Link open CAPAs with accountable owners; do not describe the recall as complete while required recall work remains.

Destroy or otherwise disposition returns only under approved instructions and documented Quality authority. Retain destruction evidence and any required authority consent.

### 4.7. Retain evidence and test readiness {#records}

Quality Unit: retain the complaint file, original report, investigation, risk decisions, contact log, reporting assessment, recall strategy, notices, reconciliation, disposition and approvals.

For US complaint records, apply the longer of one year after product expiry or one year after receipt. Apply the specific three-year distribution rule for eligible OTC products without expiry dating. Document Control must apply any longer applicable retention or legal hold.

Recall Coordinator: conduct documented exercises at the approved frequency. Use representative distribution and an unavailable-person or system scenario. Record traceability time, completeness, contact failures and corrective actions. Do not send exercise notices outside the agreed exercise participants.

Quality Unit: trend complaint rates using a relevant exposure denominator, repeat defects, late reports, missed contacts and exercise failures. Escalate serious new signals immediately; do not defer them to periodic Management Review.

## 5. Adoption and closure checklist {#checklist}

- Confirm: Out-of-hours authority, reporting clocks and distribution evidence are operationally defined.
- Confirm: Every report is recorded and triaged, with independent safety referral and no delay from missing details.
- Confirm: Containment and evolving scope are supported by evidence before final root cause is known.
- Confirm: Risk, market action and reporting are separate documented decisions without waiting for root cause.
- Confirm: The recall reaches the selected distribution depth, blocks further supply and controls returned product.
- Confirm: Complaint closure and recall closure are distinct; reconciliation and notification effectiveness support closure.
- Confirm: Records support retrospective reconstruction, and readiness failures lead to correction.
